# Building Polydeukes in Public #5 — The pivot ships, and its first measurement says we ignore our own advice

**English** · [한국어](./2026-08-v0.5-diagnostic-pivot.ko.md)

> *v0.5.0 · the diagnostic pivot becomes the shipped default: blocking narrows to the judging
> chain's self-protection, user disciplines land as advisories, and the two measurements
> post #4 promised are reported here with their denominators — including the uncomfortable
> one: 9.9% of advisories were consumed. One round of ignore-dominance, on the record.*

Post #4 ended with a falsification promise: report the pivot's two measurements, with their
denominators, or explain ourselves. v0.5.0 closed on 2026-08-28 — eight tickets, a gate
audit that attacked its own grading sheet, and the first release where the product's default
answer to a broken discipline is a recorded advisory instead of a refused call. The measurements
below fulfill that commitment, including the low advisory-consumption result.

## The promise paid: two measurements, denominators attached

**Advise consumption: 73/735 = 9.9%.** The definition: an `advised` row whose
(label, subject) pair is re-observed later as `passed` in the same session — the discipline
told you what was wrong, and a later call on the same target passed. The denominator is
every advised row since the default flipped (2026-08-24, the day POSTURE-01 landed) through
the gate session. Two limitations matter when interpreting this ratio. First, the log has no
session column, so "same session" is approximated by gaps in the row stream; widening the
window to "re-observed anywhere later" barely moves it (10.1%). Second, the denominator is
call-weighted, not decision-weighted: 95% of those advisories come from three
read-before-editing disciplines that fire on *every* call while the required reading hasn't
happened, so one ignored advisory in one session multiplies into hundreds of rows. The row count
therefore exceeds the number of decisions, though ignored advisories still predominate. This was
one round of ignore-dominance.

The interesting part is the variance. One discipline's advisories were consumed at 77%
(read the covenant package's recorded history before editing its judges — the advisory names
one file, reading it is cheap, and the next edit passes). Another sat at exactly 0% across
292 rows. Same family, same mechanism, same session surface. The difference between 77% and 0%
cannot be attributed to the enforcement level alone. It raises the question of when an agent
acts on an advisory, which is what the pivot was designed to investigate.

**Natural blocks: 162, of which 88% were the chain protecting itself.** The other gate
indicator is the true-positive rate of what still blocks. In the same window: 143 of 162
blocked rows were the judging chain's self-protection (mentions of the judge's own
executables and config during rebuild windows, plus eight assembly fail-closed refusals —
more on those below), and 19 were user disciplines that explicitly opted into blocking.
False-block reports filed in the window, measured over the dogfooding journal and the
roadmap's friction table: zero. The pivot's claim — that blocking would narrow to
self-protection plus explicit promotion — is what the log now shows.

There is a standing falsification condition attached to the consumption number: if it stays
ignore-dominant for a second consecutive round, the roadmap directs us to judge the
*delivery channel* defective before reconsidering the default posture. This is the first of those
consecutive rounds.

## The channel was measured, not assumed

We suspect the delivery channel because we tested it. The hook
protocol documents that an allow decision can carry a `reason` the model will see. A live
probe pair showed the documented channel fires and delivers nothing the agent acts on — so
the only working delivery for an advisory today is the one the installed skill teaches:
consult the telemetry log at task boundaries. That probe tested whether an advisory reaches its
*reader*, not merely whether the hook emits it. That
finding shipped inside DIAG-02 and is now, one release later, the standing explanation for
a 9.9% consumption rate: the rows are written; almost nothing reads them back yet.

## What v0.5.0 actually was

Eight tickets, one posture change.

- **`pdks explain`** — the assembled state, rendered: every registration, its routing scope,
  its placement per surface, and what compiled to a skip *with the reason* (CLI-01).
- **`why` on every block message** — the discipline's own justification travels with the
  refusal, so the agent receives both the instruction and its grounds (COVENANT-19).
- **`draft` entries** — a discipline can be registered as an id plus a why, judged by
  nothing, visible in `explain` as unpromoted. Drafts are excluded from the judging path
  (CONFIG-10).
- **Per-entry enforcement and the advise default** — an entry carries its own
  `enforce`; the lenient side of entry and surface wins; and since POSTURE-01, *omitting*
  the level means advise. Block is a promotion an author chooses, and the only things that
  block unasked are the chain's self-protections.
- **One violation, three domains** — `pdks covenant check` now judges staged changes, the
  working tree, or any ref range through the same IR and dispatcher (DIAG-01). The gate
  verified the same violation produces character-identical verdict lines in all three.
- **Consolidated dispatch** — per-registration judge processes collapsed into in-process
  calls: 170.8ms → 19.1ms per call, with byte-identical telemetry, exits, and stderr locked
  by fixtures measured *before* the switch (DISPATCH-01).
- **A skill that drafts disciplines** — `pdks init claude-code` now installs a sixth
  artifact: a skill that takes "I keep doing X" and lands it as either a judged advisory
  entry or a draft, writing the regex and proving it fires (DIAG-02).

The eighth ticket is the posture itself (POSTURE-01), and one incidental first: during the
window, a partial rebuild crashed assembly and the hook refused eight calls in 71 seconds —
each refusal recorded, zero calls passed unrecorded. Fail-closed held under assembly
failure, which is exactly the invariant it exists for.

## The gate audited its own grading sheet

The v0.4.0 gate's lesson was that the audit sheet is itself an artifact that can lie, so
this gate retained independent adversarial review: an independent auditor re-ran every cited command
and
attacked the sheet's claims. Result: zero stale numbers across everything re-measured
(1,599 tests green across five packages, the explain output, the parity suite, the
telemetry rows cited by timestamp) — and seven prose defects in the sheet itself, all of
the same species: a declared limit stated narrower than it is, a count cited without its
denominator, an attribution the log cannot actually support. All seven are corrected in the archived
record. Two gates in a row, the defects have been in the paperwork,
not the product. Independent review will continue.
